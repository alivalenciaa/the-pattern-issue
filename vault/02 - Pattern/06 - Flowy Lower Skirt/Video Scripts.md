## Video 32 — Flowy Skirt Intro

Mannequin + explanation, no crocheting yet.

[Show original dress on mannequin, point to the flowy skirt + slit] "Okay — this is the final section, and it's my favorite: the long flowy skirt with the slit. This is what gives the dress that dramatic, elegant drape. This is what it looks like finished on the original.

Here's how it works: it's actually the exact same mesh we did at the waist — those sets of one tall stitch row and one single crochet row — but this time we use taller stitches so it's longer, more open, and flowy. We start with a treble crochet, and lower down we go even taller. And this whole section is where we create the slit on the side.

Two quick things before we start: first, we're switching to a 3mm hook for this whole section, since it's an open mesh like the waist. And second, unlike the miniskirt, this part is worked in turned rows — flat, back and forth — which is what lets the slit open up. Let me show you how we start."

## Video 33 — First TR Row (Slit Begins)

Key points locked below.

"Okay, so we're starting the flowy skirt now with our 3mm hook. This first row works into the last linked double crochet row of the miniskirt, just like the very first mesh row at the waist worked into its base row.

So: chain 4 to start — and just like the mesh decrease row, we disregard that chain, which gives us our decrease at this edge. Skip the first stitch, treble crochet into the next. Then chain 1, skip 1, treble crochet — all the way across. [demonstrate, then time-lapse]

And real quick — if your stitches don't come out perfectly even at the end, don't worry about it. Just skip an extra stitch wherever you need to so it works out. It won't be noticeable.

At the end, treble crochet 2 together over the last two spots — that's the decrease on this edge. Then turn. So just like the mesh, the decrease is handled by disregarding the chain 4 at the start and the treble crochet 2 together at the end."

## Video 34 — The SC Row + Completing Sets (Decrease Sets for the Slit)

Same set logic as mesh midsection (Section 5), adapted to TR.

"Now the second row of the set — the single crochet row. This works exactly like the single crochet row from the waist mesh section. You chain your turning chain, and because we're still opening the slit, this row is also a decrease — so we decrease at the beginning and again at the end, same as we did in the mesh decrease sets.

Work single crochet into each space with a chain 1 in between — and keep those chain 1s loose, because your treble crochets will work into them on the next row. [demonstrate, then time-lapse]

Those two rows together — the treble crochet row and the single crochet row — that's one set, just like before.

🎙️ Quick note — the slit opens up a lot more than it looks like from just these two decrease sets. Once the skirt starts falling, it really opens. Honestly, one decrease set might be all you need for a moderate slit. If you want it more dramatic, two is plenty — I wouldn't go past two unless you want a really wide opening."

## Video 35 — Straight Sets Down the Upper Skirt

"Once your slit is open as much as you want, you stop decreasing and just work straight sets — same as the straight sets from the waist mesh section, just with the treble crochet.

For the treble crochet straight row, you chain 5 — because just like the mesh, we need one extra chain to count as that chain-1 space between stitches. So 4 of those chains count as your first treble crochet, and the 5th acts as the ch-1. Put your marker in the 4th chain. Then it's the same as the mesh: treble crochet 2 together over the first two chain spaces, chain 1, treble crochet into each chain space across, and at the end treble crochet 2 together over the last two chain spaces, chain 1, and a treble crochet into the last stitch.

Then the single crochet straight row is exactly the same as before — chain 3, marker in the middle chain, single crochet on top of each treble crochet with a chain 1 in between, kept loose. To finish this row, your last single crochet goes into that 4th chain of the starting chain 5 — the marked one — the same way the mesh SC row finished into its marked chain.

Keep working these straight sets down until you reach about the halfway point of the skirt — for me that's around my knee. Try it on as you go and keep it flowing straight down."

On-screen: "Slit open enough → stop decreasing" · "Straight sets: TR row + SC row, no decreases" · "TR straight row: Ch 5 (4 = TR, 5th = ch-1), marker in 4th chain" · "TR2tog first 2 ch spaces · ch 1 · TR across · TR2tog last 2 · ch 1 · TR into last stitch" · "SC straight row: Ch 3, marker in middle chain, SC + ch 1 loose across" · "Finish SC row into the marked 4th chain of the Ch 5" · "Work straight down to about the halfway point (designer: ~knee)"

## Video 36 — Lower Skirt Transition (Taller Stitch) + Finishing

"Now that I've reached about my knees, I'm going to transition into a taller stitch for the bottom half. This is totally optional — you can keep doing the same treble crochet stitch all the way down if you want. But for me, this is what I did on the original dress. Going taller here gives the bottom a longer, more open, flowy look and creates a really pretty transition as the skirt falls.

Everything works exactly the same — same sets, same structure, still working into the chain spaces, still chaining 1 in between — I'm just going one stitch taller on both rows. Instead of a treble crochet, I'll do a double treble, which is yarning over three times. And instead of the single crochet row, I'll do a double crochet row. That's the only change.

[demonstrate the double treble + the DC row]

The turning chains just bump up one, same logic as always: the double treble straight row is chain 6 with your marker in the 5th chain, and the double crochet row is chain 4 with your marker in the 3rd chain. Both rows are straight now — no more decreasing, since our slit is already open.

Now just keep working these taller sets straight down until you reach your desired length — for me that's floor length. Try it on as you go. Once you're at your length, meet me back here — because we're not quite done yet. We still have to add a pretty little ruffle trim around the slit and the bottom edge, and I'll show you that next."
