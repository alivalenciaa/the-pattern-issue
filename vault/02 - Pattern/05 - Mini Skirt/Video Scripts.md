## Video 25 — How Much to Chain (Measuring + Calculation)

Explanation + measuring tape demo. No crocheting yet.

"Before we chain anything, we need to figure out how many chains to make for the back. The measurement we're using here isn't your true waist — it's where the miniskirt actually sits on your body, which is lower, more toward the upper hip area. So measure yourself at that exact point, wherever your miniskirt will begin.

Then take your mesh front and lay it flat — don't stretch it. Measure across. [demonstrate with measuring tape on the mesh front] For me, my mesh front measures about 14 and a half inches laying flat.

Now subtract your mesh front from that lower measurement to get how much chain you need for the back. For me, my back chain ended up being about 14 and a half inches too.

The best way to find your number is to chain what you think you need, try it on, and adjust until it feels snug but not too tight — you want a little give, but not loose. My first version was a little too tight so I added 9 more chains and that felt right. So don't be afraid to adjust. Write your final number down once you're happy with the fit."

On-screen: "Measure where the miniskirt SITS on your body (not your true waist)" · "Lay mesh front flat, measure across (don't stretch)" · "Back chain = that measurement − mesh front" · "Designer: mesh front ~14.5"" · "Designer: back chain ~14.5"" · "Try it on and adjust — snug but not too tight" · "A little give is good, loose is not"

## Video 26 — Making the Foundation Chain + Joining

Real-time demo — give time to show the chaining and the join clearly.

"Okay, now let's make our chain. First, switch to your 2.5mm hook — we'll use this for the whole miniskirt.

Starting from one edge of your last mesh row, we're going to chain. [begin chaining slowly] Just keep chaining until your chain reaches the back measurement you calculated — for me that's about 14 and a half inches. Don't stretch it while you measure — chain loosely and naturally, and measure it at rest. [continue, show measuring the chain against the tape]

Okay, I've reached my measurement. Now before we join, we have to make sure the chain isn't twisted. [show running finger along chain] Run your finger along the whole chain and make sure the braided, bumpy side is facing the same way the whole length. If it's twisted anywhere, straighten it out now — because once we join it twisted, we can't fix it without undoing everything.

Now we join. Bring your chain over to the other edge of the mesh — the opposite side from where you started. [show this] Insert your hook through that edge stitch, going through it fully, and do a slip stitch: yarn over, and pull through both the stitch and the loop on your hook. [demonstrate slowly] And now your chain is joined into a full ring around your waist."

On-screen: "Switch to 2.5mm hook" · "Chain from one mesh edge until you reach your back measurement" · "Designer: ~14.5" of chain" · "Chain loosely — measure at rest, don't stretch" · "Before joining: run finger along chain, braided side same way all across" · "If twisted → fix BEFORE joining" · "Join: insert hook through other mesh edge, slip stitch"

## Video 27 — Round 1 (HDC Base Round)

Real-time demo. Builds a clean base for the LDC. Only the round-start marker is used here — side markers aren't introduced until Video 28, after this round is done.

"Now we work our first round, and this one is in half double crochet — not LDC yet. We'll get to LDC next round. The reason is that LDC needs a stitch underneath it to hold onto, so this HDC round builds that clean base first.

Right after the join, chain 1. [show] Then work your first HDC into that very same stitch where you just joined — the first stitch of the mesh. [demonstrate the first HDC slowly]

Now place a stitch marker on that first HDC. This one marks the start of your round, so you always know where each new round begins. [show placing it]

From here we keep going in one direction — we never turn. [start working] First we work across the mesh front. Because the last mesh row was a single crochet row, you'll work 1 HDC into each single crochet AND 1 HDC into each chain space — so it goes: HDC in the single crochet, HDC in the chain space, all the way across. [demonstrate slowly, then time-lapse]

Now we work across the chain part: 1 HDC into each chain, all the way across the back. [show a few, then time-lapse]

Keep going until you get back to your first marked stitch. Once you're there, work one regular DOUBLE crochet into it.

The reason we're doing a double crochet here instead of a half double crochet is because this is going to be the stitch that Round 2 links into. Linked double crochet needs something to link into for that very first stitch — so this double crochet sets that up. It's the only double crochet we'll do here. And that's the end of Round 1."

## Video 28 — Finding Increase Points + Marker Placement

"Okay, now we need to find our two increase points. These are going to be the two sides of your skirt — basically the left and right sides of your body, across from each other.

At these two points, every round, we're going to increase — meaning we'll work two linked double crochets into one stitch instead of just one. That's what gradually widens the skirt from your waist out to your hips. So go ahead and place a stitch marker at each of those two side points.

Now, just make sure your increase point isn't on the exact same stitch as where your round begins. I'm going to move mine over one stitch just to keep things clean. It doesn't make a difference whether it's one stitch before or after — as long as your two increase points are roughly on opposite sides of your body, you're good.

Also, make sure your increase markers are a different color from your round-start marker, so you don't get confused about which is which as you go.

Once your two side markers are placed, we're ready to start Round 2 — and this is where we switch to linked double crochet. Let me show you."

On-screen: "Find your 2 increase points = the 2 sides of your skirt" · "Place a marker at each side" · "Every round: 2 LDC into each marked stitch = the increase" · "This widens waist → hips" · "Keep increase points off the round-start stitch (move 1 over if needed)" · "Use a DIFFERENT color from your round-start marker" · "2 increase points = opposite sides of body"

So here's how to do a linked double crochet: [show and explain the LDC stitch slowly, step by step]

Now — since this stitch is also my increase point, I'm going to work another linked double crochet into that same stitch. [show the second LDC into the same stitch]

That's your increase — two linked double crochets into one stitch.

Now place your stitch marker on the FIRST of those two stitches you just made. [show placing marker] That way you'll always know where your increase point is next round.

Once it sits comfortably over the fullest part of your hips and butt, you're ready to stop increasing."

## Video 30 — Done Increasing + Going Straight

"Okay, I'm done with my increases now. Forget the measuring tape for this part — honestly, trying it on is the most reliable way to know when you're done. Once it fits well over your hips and butt and feels comfortable, that's your cue to stop increasing. Trust what you feel on your body.

I ended up doing about 20 increase rounds total. Yours might be more or less — it really depends on your body.

From here, remove your two side markers. You won't need them anymore. If you have a round-start marker, keep that one.

Now we just go straight — one linked double crochet into every single stitch, all the way around, every round. No increases, no shaping. Just keep going until the miniskirt reaches the length you want.

For length — you want it to end right where a miniskirt would naturally end on your body. Think of it as where a short skirt would sit before it starts to flow out. For me that's right where the bottom mesh skirt will begin. Try it on as you go and stop when it feels right."
