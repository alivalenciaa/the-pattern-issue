## Video 2 — Materials & Tools 🟢 KEEP

"Here's what you'll need:

Yarn: I'm using a sequin yarn. You can use any yarn — but if you use something different, your fit and stitch counts may look different than mine.

Two hook sizes: 2.5mm for the bra, straps, and dense areas; 3mm for the mesh sections. The smaller hook gives full coverage. The larger hook creates the open mesh look.

Also needed: stitch markers, small sharp scissors (especially for sequin yarn — you'll need them to remove sequins), yarn needle for weaving in ends, ribbon or cord for corset lacing.

Quick note about my measurements — I'm 116 pounds with a 26.3 inch waist. Mentioning this so you have a reference point. Now let's start with the bra section."

## Video 2.5 — Tension Tip 🆕 NEW CLIP

Short clip, no demo needed.

"One quick but IMPORTANT note before we start. Your tension matters a lot for this dress — but more importantly, your tension needs to stay CONSISTENT throughout the bra. Whatever tension you start with — tight, medium, loose — keep it the same from your first row to your last row. Don't start loose and gradually tighten up, or start tight and loosen up. That'll throw off the symmetry of your bra.

Quick note on stretching: this fabric won't stretch much unless your tension is very loose. So don't count on the fabric to magically stretch and fix things later — what you make is mostly what you get. The corset back and straps will give you adjustability though. Okay, let's start the bra."

On-screen: "KEEP TENSION CONSISTENT" · "Don't start loose and tighten up (or vice versa)" · "Fabric won't stretch much unless tension is loose" · "Corset + straps = your adjustability"

## Video 3 — Foundation Chain + Row 1 🎙️ VOICEOVER

🎯 Pinpoints: "28 chains" → "24 chains" · "30 total chains" → "26 total chains (24 + 2 turning)"

"We're starting with the bra section using the 2.5mm hook.

Foundation chain: Measure vertically from where you want the bottom of the bra to sit, up to where you want the top edge. That tells you how many chains to make. For me, that's 24 chains plus 2 turning chains — so 26 chains total. Your number will be different — chain enough to cover your bust height.

Sequin tip: the sequins get in the way while chaining. Just push them down the yarn as you work and chain around them.

Row 1: Skip the first 2 chains from your hook — those are your turning chain and don't count as a stitch. Insert your hook into the 3rd chain from the hook and work your first HDC. Continue HDC into each chain across.

I also like to place a stitch marker on my first stitch of every row. It's optional but it keeps the project neat and helps me track shaping later. Once you've worked HDC into every chain across, your foundation row is done."

On-screen: "Hook: 2.5mm" · "Designer's example: 24 chains + 2 turning chains" · "Skip first 2 chains, HDC into 3rd chain from hook" · "Push sequins down as you chain"

## Video 4 — Straight Back Panel Rows 🎙️ VOICEOVER

🎯 Pinpoints: "16 rows" → "18 rows" · remove "fabric will stretch over time" emphasis

"Now we're going to work the straight back panel rows. These wrap around your back before we start the V-shaping. The number of rows depends on YOUR back — your size, your tension, your yarn.

Chain 2, turn. This is your turning chain and it does NOT count as a stitch. Work your first HDC into the FIRST stitch — don't skip it. Continue HDC across all stitches to the end of the row. Keep repeating until your back panel wraps comfortably around your back.

Keep in mind: the corset back will add some length and width when we lace it. Pulling the straps tight through the corset will stretch the fabric a bit. So your back panel might look a little shorter at this stage than the final fit. Don't rely on natural stretching — unless your tension is very loose, this fabric mostly stays the size you make it. I'll be back once my straight rows are done."

On-screen: "Ch 2, turn (does NOT count as a stitch)" · "HDC into FIRST stitch (don't skip)" · "HDC across to end" · "Designer's final count: 18 straight rows" · "Corset + straps give you adjustability later"

## Video 6 — Decrease Side (V-Shaping) 🎙️ VOICEOVER

🎯 Pinpoints: "28 stitches" → "24 stitches" · "14 stitches" → "12 stitches"

"Now we're shaping the V for the bra. This is what curves over your bust. The V-shape is ONE-SIDED — all decreases happen on the SAME side every row. The opposite side stays straight.

Chain 2, turn. Instead of a regular HDC, work a half double crochet decrease — HDC2tog — over the first 2 stitches. This joins them and removes one stitch. Then continue regular HDC across to the end. No decrease on the other side. You lose 1 stitch per row.

Keep decreasing on the same side every row until you reach about HALF of your original stitch count. For me, I started with 24 stitches, so I'll decrease until I have 12 stitches. Keep your stitch marker on the first stitch of each row so you always know which side has the decreases. I'll be back when my decreases are done."

On-screen: "V-shape is ONE-SIDED" · "All decreases on the SAME side every row" · "Ch 2 → HDC2tog over first 2 → HDC across" · "Lose 1 stitch per row" · "Stop at HALF your starting count" · "Designer: 24 → 12 stitches"

## Video 7 — Increase Side (V-Shaping) 🎙️ VOICEOVER

🎯 Pinpoints: "from 14 back to 28" → "from 12 back to 24"

"Now we mirror the decreases by increasing back up to our original stitch count. All increases happen on the SAME side as the decreases. Opposite side still stays straight.

Chain 2, turn. Work 2 HDC into the FIRST stitch — that's your increase. Then continue regular HDC across to the end. You gain 1 stitch per row. Keep increasing until you're back to your original stitch count. For me, I'm going from 12 back up to 24.

You need to work the SAME number of increase rows as decrease rows so the V is symmetrical. Once you're back to your starting count, the V is done. Next, we work straight rows to mirror the beginning."

On-screen: "Increases on the SAME side as decreases" · "Ch 2 → 2 HDC into first stitch → HDC across" · "Gain 1 stitch per row" · "Same number of increase rows as decrease rows" · "Designer: 12 → 24 stitches"

## Video 8 — Post-Shaping Straight Rows 🎙️ VOICEOVER

🎯 Pinpoints: row count → "18 straight rows"

"Now that the V is complete, we work straight rows again to mirror the beginning of the bra. Whatever number of straight rows you did at the start, do the same number now. This creates symmetry. For me, I did 18 straight rows at the beginning, so I'll do 18 now. Same technique as before — ch 2, turn, HDC into first stitch, HDC across. This completes one full side of the bra."

On-screen: "Mirror your beginning straight rows" · "Same number of rows = symmetry" · "Designer: 18 straight rows to match"

## Video 9 — Final Fit Check + Optional Adjustment 🆕 NEW CLIP

Film with bra on the body.

"One full side of the bra is done. Before we move to the corset rows, let's do a fit check. [Show holding panel against body] If your back panel feels like it wraps comfortably and the V sits where you want it on your chest — you're good to move on.

But if it still feels too short or doesn't wrap around your back enough, you can add MORE straight rows on both ends of the bra to extend the back panel. Whatever number of extra rows you add to one side, add the SAME number to the other side. The bra needs to stay symmetrical. For me, no adjustments needed — the corset rows will complete the fit perfectly. Either way, next we'll work the corset rows."

## Video 13 — V-Neckline Edging 🟢 KEEP

🎯 Pinpoint (minor): mention both corset sides should be done before this step

"Now that both corset sides are complete, we'll add a clean edge along the V-neckline at the top of the bra. This is a simple single crochet edging that gives the V a polished, finished look and adds stability.

Reattach yarn at one corner of the V-neckline. Work 1 SC evenly spaced across the entire top edge of the panel, following the V-shape. This is NOT stitch-count specific — just work enough SCs to keep the edge smooth and flat. Adjust your spacing so it doesn't pucker or flare.

Fasten off when you reach the other side. The bra panel is now complete! Next we'll move on to attaching the straps."

On-screen: "Both corset sides should be done first" · "Reattach yarn at one corner of V-neckline" · "SC evenly across top edge" · "Not stitch-count specific" · "Keep edge smooth and flat" · "Fasten off when done — bra panel complete"
