## Video 20 — First Decrease Set

**Part A — First DC decrease row (into HDC stitches):**

"This first mesh row is a transition row — we're working into the HDC stitches from our coverage rows. Chain 3 — disregarded, not counted as a stitch. Stitch marker on the first DC you make. Skip the first HDC, DC into the next. Ch 1, skip 1, DC into the next. Repeat across. Keep your ch-1 spaces loose. When you have 4 stitches left: ch 1, skip 1, start a DC but don't finish it (2 loops on hook), YO, skip 1, insert into last stitch, pull up a loop (4 loops), YO pull through 2, YO pull through all 3. That's your DC2tog."

**Part B — First SC decrease row:**

"Chain 2. Stitch marker on the first SC you make. The ch 2 is the decrease on this side. Single crochet into the next double crochet — not the one right next to the chain 2, but the one after it. Ch 1 (keep loose), SC into the next DC. Repeat across. When you have 2 DCs left, SC2tog: insert into next DC pull up loop, insert into last DC pull up loop (3 loops), YO pull through all 3. Those 2 rows together = one decrease set."

**Part C — Second DC decrease row (into chain spaces):**

🎙️ Voiceover at the START of this clip: "Quick heads up — I ended up unraveling back to just one decrease set, but I'm leaving this in for those who need two or more. Only decrease as much as YOUR body needs."

"From the second set onward, the DC decrease row works into chain spaces instead of HDC stitches. Ch 3 (disregarded), marker on first DC. Skip the first chain space, DC into the next chain space. Ch 1, DC into next chain space. Repeat across. At the end, DC2tog over the second-to-last chain space and the last SC from the previous row — the marked stitch. (Because the previous row was a decrease SC row, its ch-2 is disregarded — there's no middle chain to work into.)"

**Part D — Second SC decrease row (completes the full second set):**

"And now the single crochet row to complete the second decrease set — same as before. Chain 2 (disregarded), marker on first SC made. Single crochet into the next DC — not the one attached to the chain 2, the one after it. Chain 1 (keep loose), SC into the next DC. Repeat across. End with SC2tog over the last 2 DCs. That's your second complete decrease set.

From here, keep doing decrease sets until the mesh reaches the sides of your waist. Once you're there, head to the next video where I'll show you the straight sets. But first — a quick update about what I did."

On-screen: "Ch 2 disregarded · marker on first SC · SC into next DC · ch 1 (keep loose) · end: SC2tog" · "2nd decrease set complete ✓"

**Part E — "I unraveled" clip (comes after the full second set is shown):**

## Video 21 — Straight Set

**Part A — First DC straight row (after decrease SC row):**

"Chain 4. First 3 chains count as a DC, 4th chain acts as ch-1. Marker in the 3rd chain. DC2tog over the first 2 chain spaces (closes the gap). Ch 1, then DC into each chain space with ch 1 between, across. At the end: DC2tog over last 2 chain spaces, ch 1, DC into the last stitch. Because the row before was a DECREASE SC row, there's no middle chain — you work into that last marked SC."

On-screen: "Ch 4 (3 chains = DC, 4th = ch-1)" · "Marker in 3rd chain" · "DC2tog over first 2 chain spaces" · "End: DC2tog last 2 spaces, ch 1, DC into last marked SC" · "First straight row only: NO middle chain"

**Part B — SC straight row:**

"Chain 3 — marker in the middle/2nd chain, this acts as your first single crochet. SC on top of the next DC. Chain 1 — keep it loose — SC on top of the next. Repeat all the way across. Finish with SC on top of the last DC."

On-screen: "Ch 3 (middle/2nd chain = first SC)" · "Stitch marker in 2nd chain" · "SC on top of each DC, ch 1 between — keep LOOSE" · "Finish: SC on top of last DC"

**Part C — Ongoing DC straight row (after straight SC row):**

"Same start: ch 4, marker in 3rd chain, DC2tog over first 2 chain spaces, ch 1, DC into each chain space across. But this time at the end — because the row before was a STRAIGHT SC row — you DO work into the middle chain: DC2tog over last 2 chain spaces, ch 1, DC into the middle chain of the previous SC row."

On-screen: "Same start as before" · "End: DC2tog last 2 spaces, ch 1, DC into MIDDLE chain" · "Previous row was straight SC → middle chain is now your last stitch"

**Part D — Quick flash of ongoing SC row + flash forward + try-on overlay:**

## Video 22 — Increase Set

**Part A — Intro (voiceover):**

🎙️ "Okay, I finished my straight sets and now I'm ready to start increasing. Quick tip before you start — stretch your mesh down gently, because once the skirt is attached the weight will naturally pull it down. You want to start increasing once you've passed your waist — so the mesh can flare back out and create that hourglass shape. For me that point was right below my ribcage. Let me show you how."

On-screen: "Stretch mesh down gently before you start" · "Start increasing once you've passed your waist" · "Goal: hourglass shape"

**Part B — First DC increase row:**

"Chain 4, marker in 3rd chain. Instead of skipping the first chain space like the straight row, DC INTO it — that's the increase on this side. Ch 1, DC into each chain space across.

Ch 1 one more time, then DC into that last marked stitch, which is the middle chain. And that's your row done."

## Video 23 — Finished Mesh + Scooting Tip + Transition

"Okay, I'm back — this is what my mesh midsection looks like with all my increase sets done. One thing I noticed — on the side where you finish your DC rows (DC into last chain space then DC into middle chain or last SC), those two can sit too close together and make that edge look tighter. Don't unravel — since it's in a chain space, just gently scoot that stitch over to create more space. This applies to all your DC rows on that finishing side. Now the mesh midsection is complete — we're moving on to the miniskirt!"
