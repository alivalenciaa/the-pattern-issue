### Core Design Principles
- Made-to-measure, not size-graded. All stitch counts and row numbers are designer examples, never rules.
- Try it on constantly. Trying on is more reliable than the measuring tape — the tape read ~16" when the actual fit was correct at a target of 17.5".
- Tension must stay consistent throughout the bra. Fabric doesn't stretch much unless tension is very loose; the corset and straps provide adjustability.
- Videos are narrated with voiceover/talking. Designer's face is never on camera, so any spoken number can be fixed with a voiceover without refilming.

### Key Construction Decisions Worth Remembering
- Mesh = sets. Every mesh set is 1 tall-stitch row + 1 SC row. This same structure is reused in both the midsection (DC) and the flowy skirt (TR, then Double Treble).
- Turning chain logic: decrease rows use a disregarded turning chain; straight rows add one extra chain to act as the ch-1 spacer, with the marker in the second-to-last chain. This scales up as stitches get taller:
  - DC: decrease Ch 3 → straight Ch 4 (marker 3rd chain)
  - TR: decrease Ch 4 → straight Ch 5 (marker 4th chain)
  - Double Treble: straight Ch 6 (marker 5th chain)
- SC rows never increase. They decrease in decrease sets and stay straight in both straight and increase sets.
- Keep all ch-1 spaces loose on SC rows — tall stitches get worked into them next row.
- The "scoot" fix: on the side where DC/TR rows finish, the last two stitches can sit too close and pull tight. Gently scoot the second-to-last stitch over within its chain space. No unraveling needed.
- LDC needs an anchor: the miniskirt's Round 1 is HDC (a clean base), and Round 1 ends with a single DC so Round 2's first linked stitch has something to link into.
- Miniskirt is worked in a continuous spiral — no turning, no joins between rounds. Only the final round is closed with a slip stitch, placed at the chosen slit-start spot.
- The slit determines where the miniskirt ends: try the dress on, mark where you want the slit to begin, LDC to that stitch, slip stitch there. The yarn then sits exactly where the flowy skirt begins.
- Straps: slip stitch construction, not SC chains — SC is too bulky for the eyelets.
- Retired ideas: center-back V-dip (from the original dress) was dropped. The center-back increase now appears only as a brief optional tip, not part of the core method.

## THE FULL PATTERN

The complete written pattern, all 7 sections in order, exactly as it stands.

### Materials & Setup

**Yarn**
Sequin yarn (any color). Tutorial uses brown with gold sequins; original is pink.

**Hooks**
- 2.5 mm — bra panel, corset rows, straps
- 3 mm — mesh sections (midsection + skirt)

**Notions**
Stitch markers, yarn needle, small sharp scissors (sequin removal), ribbon/cord for corset lacing.

> [!info] Designer's measurements: 116 lbs, 26.3" waist (reference only)

> [!tip] Tension: Keep it consistent throughout. The fabric won't stretch much unless tension is very loose — the corset and straps provide adjustability.

### Section 1 — Bust / Top Panel
✓ FINAL — Worked flat, 2.5mm hook, one-sided V-shaping.

**Foundation Chain**
Chain enough to cover from bottom to top of bust. Designer: 24 chains + 2 turning = 26 total.

**Row 1**
Ch 2 (not a stitch), skip first 2 chains, HDC into 3rd chain from hook, HDC across. Designer: 24 HDC.

**Straight Back Panel Rows**
Ch 2, turn, HDC into first stitch, HDC across. Designer: 18 rows.

**Decrease Side (V-shape)**
Ch 2, turn, HDC2tog over first 2 stitches, HDC across. Opposite side stays straight. Decrease to half your count. Designer: 24 → 12 stitches (12 rows).

**Increase Side**
Ch 2, turn, 2 HDC in first stitch, HDC across. Same side as decreases. Designer: 12 → 24 stitches (12 rows).

**Post-Shaping Straight Rows**
Mirror the beginning. Designer: 18 rows.

> [!tip] After both sides, fit check. Add rows to BOTH sides if too short (keep symmetrical).

### Section 2 — Corset Back Setup
✓ FINAL — Worked along short edges. Both sides done before V-neckline.

**Hole Row (Designer: 24 stitches)**
Ch 1, turn, SC in first st, ch 3, skip 3, SC in next st, work 5 SC repeat pattern, SC in last st. Result: 3 evenly spaced holes.

**Reinforcement Row**
Ch 1, turn, SC in each SC, 3 SC into each ch-3 space. Fasten off.

Then repeat both rows on the second short edge. Fasten off.

> [!tip] Structure is fixed; spacing adjusts to your stitch count. You may have 1–3 stitches left at the end — just SC into each.

### Section 3 — V-Neckline Edging
✓ FINAL — Worked after both corset sides.

Reattach yarn at one corner of the V. Work 1 SC evenly across the top edge, following the V-shape. Not stitch-count specific — keep it smooth and flat. Fasten off. Bust panel complete.

### Section 4 — Straps
✓ FINAL — Wrap-around, adjustable, 2.5mm hook.

**Placement**
Hold bra to chest, mark attachment point, fold in half to mirror on the other side. Closer together = push-up; farther apart = comfort.

**Construction**
Attach yarn at marked stitch. Chain a long foundation, then slip stitch back along each chain toward the bra. Fasten off. Designer: 500 chains per strap.

**Adjusting**
Try on. If too short: reattach at tip, add chains, slip stitch back, fasten off.

**Sequin Removal (sequin yarn only)**
Carefully cut sequins from most of the strap + around corset holes. Cut through center of each sequin — never the yarn.

### Section 5 — Mesh Midsection
✓ FINAL — 3mm hook for the entire section.

**Mesh Structure**
Worked in SETS. Each set = 1 DC row + 1 SC row. Three phases: decrease → straight → increase.

| Set | DC Row | SC Row |
|---|---|---|
| Decrease | DC decrease | SC decrease |
| Straight | DC straight | SC straight |
| Increase | DC increase | SC straight |

**Turning Chains**

| Row | Chain | Marker |
|---|---|---|
| DC decrease | Ch 3 (disregarded) | First DC |
| DC straight/increase | Ch 4 (= DC + ch-1) | 3rd chain |
| SC decrease | Ch 2 (disregarded) | First SC |
| SC straight/increase | Ch 3 (= first SC) | Middle/2nd chain |

**Coverage Rows**
Attach yarn to bottom edge of bra. The LAST coverage row is always a decrease row (HDC2tog at start + end). 1 row min for clean base; 2+ if bra sits high. Designer: 72 HDC, 2 rows.

**DC Decrease Row**
First time (into HDC stitches): Ch 3 (disregarded), skip 1, DC into next, ch 1, skip 1, DC across, end with DC2tog over last 2 spots. From 2nd set on (into chain spaces): Ch 3, skip first ch space, DC into next, ch 1, DC into next ch space, end DC2tog over 2nd-to-last ch space + last marked SC.

**SC Decrease Row**
Ch 2 (disregarded), SC into next DC (skip the one attached to ch-2), ch 1, SC into next DC across, end SC2tog over last 2 DCs.

**DC Straight Row**
Ch 4, DC2tog over first 2 ch spaces, ch 1, DC into next ch space across, DC2tog over last 2 ch spaces, ch 1, DC into last stitch. First straight row → into last marked SC (no middle chain). Ongoing → into middle chain.

**SC Straight Row**
Ch 3 (marker in middle chain), SC on top of next DC, ch 1 across, finish SC on last DC.

**DC Increase Row**
Ch 4, DC into FIRST ch space (increase), ch 1, DC into next ch space across, DC into LAST ch space (increase), ch 1, DC into middle chain.

**SC Row in Increase Set**
Same as SC straight row — no shaping. Only the DC row increases.

> [!tip] Finishing tip: If the DC-finishing side looks tighter, gently scoot the second-to-last DC over within the chain space to even it out. No unraveling needed. Applies to all DC rows.

> [!tip] Keep all ch-1 spaces loose on SC rows — you'll work DCs into them next row.

> [!info] Designer's totals: 72 HDC · 2 coverage rows · 1 decrease set · 6 straight sets · 8 increase sets · 32 total mesh rows

### Section 6 — Miniskirt (Butt Coverage Panel)
⏳ IN PROGRESS — Worked in the round in Linked Double Crochet (LDC). 2.5mm hook throughout (foundation chain, Round 1 HDC, and all LDC rounds).

**Why Linked Double Crochet (LDC)**
LDC gives maximum coverage, a dense fabric-like texture with minimal holes, and strong shaping control — perfect for a non-sheer panel that hugs the body over the butt and upper hips.

**Construction Overview**
The miniskirt begins directly from the last mesh increase row — it's already attached, so there's no separate join to the mesh. Switch back to the 2.5mm hook for the entire miniskirt section (foundation chain, Round 1 HDC, and all LDC rounds). The mesh covers the front, so you add a foundation chain for the BACK to complete the full waist circumference, then work LDC in continuous rounds (no turning, no seam). Side increases sculpt from waist to hips; after the fullest part of the butt you can decrease slightly and/or work straight to the length you want.

**Step 1 — Foundation Chain (Back Portion)**
From the edge of your last mesh row, chain enough to cover the BACK of your body where the skirt sits. Note: this is NOT your true waist measurement — it's lower, more toward the upper hip area, wherever the miniskirt actually begins on your body.

How to calculate: Measure your mesh front laying flat (unstretched). Subtract that from your body measurement at the point where the skirt sits. Chain roughly that amount, try it on, and adjust until it feels snug but not tight — a little give is good, loose is not.

> [!info] Designer's example: Mesh front ~14.5" (flat) + back chain ~14.5" = ~29" where the skirt sits (lower waist/upper hip area). Hip: 35". Note: this measurement is NOT the true waist — it's where the miniskirt actually begins on the body.

> [!tip] Why unstretched? If you chain to a stretched measurement, the skirt sits too loose at rest. Aim for snug but not tight — a little give is good. Try it on and adjust before committing to your chain length.

**Step 1b — Join Into a Ring**
Before joining, check the chain isn't twisted — run your finger along it and make sure the braided side faces the same direction the whole way. Then join the end of your chain to the opposite edge of the mesh with a slip stitch, forming a complete ring around your waist.

**Step 2 — Work Round 1 (HDC Base Round)**
Ch 1, then work your first HDC into the same stitch where you joined (the first mesh stitch). Place a round-start marker on that first HDC — this tracks where each round begins. Keep going in ONE direction — do not turn at any point.

Work across the mesh first: because the last mesh row was an SC row, work 1 HDC into each SC AND 1 HDC into each chain space (HDC in SC, HDC in chain space, alternating across). Then continue across the back: 1 HDC into each chain. When you return to your first marked stitch, work 1 regular DC into it — this DC finishes Round 1 AND gives Round 2's first LDC something to link into.

> [!tip] Only 1 marker so far: 🔴 the round-start marker, placed on the first HDC of Round 1. The two side/increase markers aren't placed yet — those come after Round 1, once you can try the piece on and decide where your two sides should be (see Step 3).

> [!tip] Why HDC first, then a DC to finish? LDC needs a stitch below it to "link into." Round 1 in HDC creates a clean base, and the single DC at the end gives the first LDC of Round 2 its anchor.

**Step 3 — Finding Your Increase Points + Round 2 Onward (LDC + Side Increases)**
Once Round 1 is done, find your two increase points — the two sides of your skirt, roughly opposite each other on your body. Place a stitch marker at each. (Keep them off the exact round-start stitch; move one over a stitch if needed — before or after doesn't matter.)

Start Round 2 by linking your first LDC into that finishing DC, then work 1 LDC into each stitch around. At each side marker: work 2 LDC into the marked stitch (increase), then move the marker up to the first of those 2 new stitches. Keep repeating increase rounds until the piece stretches to half your hip circumference across.

> [!info] Designer's target: Hip 35" → stop increasing once the piece stretches to ~17.5" across.

**Step 4 — When to Stop Increasing**
Try the skirt on every few rounds as you increase. Stop increasing once it fits well and comfortably over the fullest part of your hips and butt. Trying it on is more reliable than measuring — the tape can read smaller than the actual stretched fit, so trust how it feels and looks on your body.

> [!info] Designer's example: ~20 increase rounds total.

**Step 5 — Straight Rounds to Length**
Once you're done increasing, remove your two side markers (keep the round-start marker). Then work straight: 1 LDC into every stitch, all the way around, every round — no increases, no decreases. Continue until the miniskirt reaches the length you want.

Where to end: Work until the miniskirt ends where a short skirt would naturally sit on your body — right before the flowy bottom skirt begins. Try it on as you go and stop when it feels right.

> [!tip] Running out of yarn? Just reattach and keep going — any reattach method works.

**Step 6 — Finishing the Section**
Once you reach your desired miniskirt length, try the dress on and decide where you want your side slit to begin. Place a stitch marker at that spot. Work LDC until you reach that marked stitch, then slip stitch into it to close off your final round. The miniskirt section is complete, and your yarn is now positioned exactly where the slit will start.

> [!info] 🟡 Designer's numbers: Back chain: ~14.5" · Mesh front: ~14.5" · Increase rounds: ~20 · Straight rounds: 🟡 ___ (worked to desired length) · Total rounds: 🟡 ___ · Length: 🟡 ___ in

> [!tip] No seam, no turn: Work in one continuous spiral the whole way — no turning, and no slip-stitch joins between rounds (only the very last round is closed with a single slip stitch at the slit spot). This gives LDC its clean, compact, non-see-through finish.

### Section 7 — Flowy Bottom Skirt with Side Slit
✓ FINAL — Open "ladder" mesh worked in the same set structure as the mesh midsection (Section 5), but with taller stitches. Worked in turned rows (flat) so the slit can open. 3mm hook (switch up from the 2.5mm miniskirt).

**The Idea**
This section reuses the exact mesh logic from Section 5 (sets of 1 tall-stitch row + 1 SC row, worked into chain spaces, with the same decrease and straight mechanics) — just with taller stitches for a longer, more open, flowier drape. It's worked flat in turned rows so one side can open into a slit.

**Picking Up From the Miniskirt**
The slit sits on one side, positioned toward the front of the leg (designer: front of the left leg). Your yarn is already positioned at your chosen slit-start spot from the end of the miniskirt (Step 6) — that's where this section begins.

**Phase 1 — Upper Skirt (Treble Crochet sets)**
Tall stitch = Treble Crochet (TR): yarn over twice. This section uses the exact same row structure as the mesh midsection (Section 5) — same turning-chain and decrease logic — just with TR (Ch 4) in place of DC (Ch 3), because TR is taller.

TR Decrease Row — first time (into the LDC edge): Ch 4 (disregarded = the decrease on this edge), skip 1, TR into next, ch 1, skip 1, TR across, end with TR2tog over the last 2 spots. From the 2nd set on (into chain spaces): Ch 4 (disregarded), skip first ch space, TR into next, ch 1, TR into next ch space, end TR2tog over the 2nd-to-last ch space + last marked SC.

SC Decrease Row — identical to the mesh: Ch 2 (disregarded), SC into next TR (skip the one attached to the ch-2), ch 1, SC into next TR across, end SC2tog over the last 2 TRs.

Each TR row + SC row = one set. Work decrease sets (both rows decrease) until the slit is open as much as you want (designer: ~🟡 TBD sets — try it on).

Then switch to straight sets:

TR Straight Row: Ch 5 (4 chains count as the TR + the 5th acts as ch-1), marker in the 4th chain. TR2tog over the first 2 ch spaces, ch 1, TR into next ch space across, TR2tog over the last 2 ch spaces, ch 1, TR into last stitch. (First straight row → into last marked SC, no middle chain. Ongoing → into middle chain.)

SC Straight Row: Ch 3 (marker in middle chain), SC on top of next TR, ch 1 across, finish SC on last TR, and the final SC goes into the marked 4th chain of the starting Ch 5.

Work straight sets down to about the halfway point of the skirt (designer: ~knee).

**Phase 2 — Lower Skirt (taller transition, ~knee down)**
About halfway down (designer: ~knee), you can transition to taller stitches for a longer, more open, flowy drape. This is optional — you can keep the treble crochet all the way down if you prefer. Same set structure, still worked into chain spaces with ch 1 between, both rows straight (no decreasing — the slit is already open). The changes: tall stitch becomes a Double Treble (yarn over three times) and the SC row becomes a DC row.

Turning chains (bump up one, same logic): Double Treble straight row → Ch 6, marker in the 5th chain. DC row → Ch 4, marker in the 3rd chain. Work these sets straight down to your final length (designer: floor length, height 5'5").

**Finishing Phase 2**
Work the taller sets straight down until you reach your desired length (designer: floor length). Don't fasten off yet — the dress isn't finished. Next comes a ruffle trim worked around the slit edges and the bottom hem.

**Ruffle Border (Slit + Bottom Edge)**
The ruffle is a single row worked all the way around the entire opening: along the whole bottom edge, up one side of the slit, and back down the other side. No foundation row needed — the ruffle is worked directly into the existing chain spaces and row-end gaps.

To start: Ch 3 and turn your work.

Bottom edge: Work 7 TR into each chain space all the way across. Packing that many stitches into one space is what forces the fabric to bunch and ruffle.

Up the slit sides: The gaps here are row-ends, and they vary in size because the row heights changed as the skirt went down. Adjust as you go — more stitches in the bigger gaps, fewer in the smaller ones. There's no exact count to follow: just fill each gap enough that it bunches up and ruffles.

> [!info] Designer's counts: 7 TR in each bottom-edge chain space · ~10 TR in the biggest slit gaps · ~6–7 TR in the smaller gaps.

To finish: When you come all the way back around to where you started, slip stitch into the top of your very first treble crochet. Fasten off and weave in all ends. The dress is complete.

> [!tip] Don't overthink the counts. If a section looks flat, it needed more stitches; if it's bunching too hard, use fewer. The goal is an even ruffle all the way around.

> [!info] 🟡 Designer's numbers: Slit decrease sets: 2 · Straight sets to knee: 12 (14 sets total to the knee) · Lower-skirt (Double Treble) sets: 🟡 ___ · Total length: 🟡 ___ (floor length)

> [!tip] Slit note: The slit opens up far more than it appears during the decrease sets — once the skirt lengthens and falls, it really opens. 1 decrease set is recommended for a moderate slit. 2 gives a more dramatic opening (this is what the designer did). Going past 2 will give a very wide opening.

> [!tip] Same mechanics as Section 5: turning chains, working into chain spaces, keeping SC ch-1 spaces loose, and the finishing "scoot" tip all carry over — just taller stitches.
